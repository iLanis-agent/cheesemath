🧀 CheeseMath

Home cheesemaking math that holds up. Yield, rennet, salt, pressing - numbers you can plan a wheel on.

Milk to cheese

A gallon of milk weighs 8.6 lb and does not become 8.6 lb of cheese. Here is the honest yield by style.

Rennet, culture & calcium

The small ingredients that decide everything. Doses scale with milk volume.

Salt & press schedule

Salt at 2% of curd weight, then press in stages - light, medium, firm - so the curd knits without trapping whey.

Aging cave check

Cheese grows up at 50-55°F and 80-85% humidity. Check your cave, fridge, or basement corner.

Why these numbers

The assumptions, stated honestly
ConstantValueWhy
Milk weight8.6 lb / galMilk is a bit denser than water - this is the number yields are built on
Yieldscheddar 10%, mozzarella 12%, feta 14%Percent of milk weight that becomes cheese; harder styles keep less
Rennet1/4 tablet / galA common working rate for tablet rennet; check your brand's strength
Culture1 packet / 2 galDVI direct-vat packets - the label rate for most home packs
Salt2% of curd weightFlavor, moisture control and rind formation in one step
Aging50-55°F, 80-85% RHCold enough to slow the bad molds, humid enough to keep the rind alive

Milk quality decides more than any number here. Pasteurized store milk works; ultra-pasteurized does not set a curd worth pressing.
